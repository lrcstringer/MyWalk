What is the Freedom Plan?

The Freedom Plan is a structured, self-guided programme built into the MyWalk app for users working to change a deeply ingrained behavioural pattern — commonly pornography use, compulsive eating, substance misuse, gambling, or similar. It runs inside a "breaking habits" category of the app's habit system.

The programme has no fixed end date. It spans a minimum of ninety days before its highest phase is reached, and continues indefinitely with quarterly reviews thereafter. It is designed to be worked through in small, daily increments: most activities take two to five minutes.

The clinical approach draws from Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy (identifying and challenging automatic thoughts), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (values clarification, urge surfing), and Relapse Prevention Therapy (cue identification, high-risk situation planning, lapse response). The AVE (Abstinence Violation Effect) concept from relapse prevention is taught explicitly to users.

How a user starts

When a user adds a "Breaking Free" habit in the app, the Freedom Plan is attached automatically. On starting, the user names the pattern they are working on. This name (and a machine-determined "habit type") is used throughout the programme to personalise cue suggestions and questions.

All responses the user writes are encrypted end-to-end and stored privately. Counsellors do not receive data from the app automatically — the app is a private self-help tool, and what a user shares with a counsellor is their own choice.

Phase Progression

The programme has four phases. The app automatically moves the user forward when the conditions below are met — the user does not choose their phase. Phase is primarily time-and-progress gated; it cannot be accelerated by completing activities faster.

Phase Name Trigger condition Focus
Phase 1 Getting Started Default — every user begins here Awareness: daily check-ins, logging moments, building honesty habits
Phase 2 Going Deeper Cue map completed or 14 days since starting or 14 daily check-ins logged Pattern analysis: thought examination, cue mapping, values inventory
Phase 3 Sustained Practice 30 days since starting or any lapse recorded Consolidation: coping plan building, lifestyle audit, lapse navigation
Phase 4 Maintenance 90 days since starting and values inventory completed Long-term sustainability: quarterly reviews, compass check-ins
Clinical note: A lapse before day 30 moves the user immediately from Phase 1 to Phase 3 — skipping Phase 2's time gate. This is intentional: a lapse is treated as a significant event that unlocks the lapse navigation tools and the full support framework immediately, rather than waiting for a calendar milestone.

Module Unlocking

Modules 2, 4, and 5 are gated — they become accessible only when specific conditions are met. Modules 1 and 3 are always available from day one.

Module Name Available when Rationale
M1 Know Your Pattern Always unlocked Foundation — logging and check-ins begin immediately
M2 Challenge Your Thinking Cue map completed or day 28+ Thought examination works best after cue awareness is established; time gate prevents skipping ahead
M3 Anchor to Your Values Always unlocked Values clarification can begin at any stage; the Weekly Compass is most meaningful after a values inventory is done
M4 Build Your Guardrails Cue map completed or day 28+ Coping plans are seeded from the cue map — they need specific cues to be effective
M5 Navigate Lapses Day 30+ or any lapse recorded Lapse tools are available earlier if the user experiences a lapse; AVE education is given before letter writing
Module 1

Know Your Pattern

🔓 Always available

Module 1 is the foundation of the programme. Its core premise is that lasting change requires honest self-observation before strategy. Users are asked to log moments honestly before they are guided toward analysis. The cue map — the culmination of Module 1 — is the keystone that unlocks Modules 2 and 4.

Daily Check-In

The Daily Check-In is available every day and is the primary recurring activity of Module 1. It takes approximately one minute. The app counts these check-ins and uses the count toward phase and module unlock conditions.

What the user is asked

Question 1 — Wellbeing
How are you doing emotionally today?
Slider: 1 (Not great) → 10 (Really well)
Question 2 — Pattern encounter
Did you encounter your pattern today?
Yes, I slipped I felt the urge but didn't act Clear day

What the app does in response

  • Slipped: Offers the choice to open the Behaviour Log immediately, or save without logging. Either way, the check-in is recorded.
  • Urge only: Offers the choice to open Thought Examination (Module 2) for the thought that arose, or save without it.
  • Clear day: Saves automatically with a brief affirming message.
  • Rating ≤ 3: After saving, the app shows an inline message: "It looks like it's been a difficult stretch. Stressful periods are when the plan matters most. Is your coping plan still working?" — with a link to the High-Risk Situation plans in Module 4.

The check-in data (emotional rating and outcome) is stored privately. The user's check-in history is visible in the Freedom Journey tab.

Behaviour Log

The Behaviour Log is used when the user has experienced a pattern occurrence — either through the Daily Check-In's "Yes, I slipped" route, or by tapping "Log a moment" directly on their habit card at any time. It captures the ABCs of a specific moment: Antecedents, Behaviour context, and Cognitions. Users are encouraged to log within minutes of a moment, while detail is fresh.

Clinical significance: The app monitors whether the user has logged at least 5 moments. Below 5 logs, the Cue Map activity will not be surfaced, even if the user has been using the app for 14+ days. The cue map needs sufficient raw data to be meaningful.

The four-step logging flow

  1. Situation
    Where were you? What time of day was it?
    e.g. Home alone, 11pm / Office, after a difficult meeting
  2. Preceding activity
    What were you doing immediately before?
    e.g. Browsing my phone / Just got home from work / Couldn't sleep
  3. Emotional state
    What were you feeling emotionally?
    Free-text emotion name (e.g. lonely, anxious, bored) plus a slider: How intense was it? (0 = Mild → 10 = Overwhelming)
  4. Preceding thought
    What thought arose just before? Even a single sentence is fine.
    e.g. "I deserve this." / "Just for a minute." / "No one will know."

After saving, the app affirms: "Logged. Every honest record brings you closer to understanding your pattern." The log entry is saved in draft form while the user is mid-completion, so it is not lost if they navigate away.

Mid-Point Reflection

The Mid-Point Reflection surfaces as a pending action between Day 8 and Day 10 of the programme (after the user has had a week to log moments but before the two-week cue map trigger). It does not re-appear once completed. It is a brief, no-data-entry reflection before analysis begins.

The app's introduction: "You've been logging moments for a little while now. Before looking for patterns, just take a few minutes to reflect honestly."

Three questions asked

Step 1
Looking at what you've logged so far — does anything surprise you?
Even if you haven't noticed a pattern yet, that's worth noting.
Step 2
Is there a time of day, place, or emotional state that seems to come up more than once?
Don't force a pattern — just notice if anything stands out.
Step 3
How are you feeling about this process so far?
Honest is good. Frustrated is fine. There are no wrong answers here.

Completion message: "Reflection saved. Keep logging. The pattern will become clearer with more data."

Cue Map (Cue Hierarchy)

The Cue Map is the most substantive activity in Module 1 — and arguably the most clinically important in the whole programme. It surfaces as a pending action from Day 14 onwards, provided the user has logged at least 5 moments. It produces a ranked list of 2–6 trigger patterns in the user's own words, which is then used throughout Modules 2, 4, and 5.

When it surfaces (gating)

ConditionWhat the app does
Day 14+, fewer than 5 logs, before Day 21Shows message: "X moments logged so far — a few more will help build a more accurate map." Does not open the activity.
Day 21–27, fewer than 5 logsShows a gentle nudge about logging being tricky. Does not open the activity.
Day 28+, fewer than 5 logsOpens the activity with lighter-touch headings — the cue map proceeds with whatever logs exist.
Day 14+, 5 or more logsOpens the full cue map activity.

Five stages of the Cue Map

  1. Entry — Acknowledgement
    A reflective screen that acknowledges the user's honesty in logging: "You've been logging moments for two weeks. That takes honesty and courage. Now let's look at what your logs are telling you — together."
  2. Review — Reading your logs
    The user is shown all their Behaviour Log entries in chronological order. Each entry shows the date, the emotion logged, the situation, and the preceding thought (truncated; tappable to expand). The instruction: "Read through what you've captured. Don't analyse yet — just notice." The user taps "I've read through them" to continue.
  3. AI Pattern Suggestion — Candidate cues
    The app sends the user's log entries to a server-side AI analysis function. The AI identifies 2–4 cue patterns specific to the log content and the user's habit type, formatted as plain-language phrases under 12 words each (e.g. "Late evenings when feeling flat or stressed"). These are shown as candidate cards — the user can keep or remove each one. If the AI call fails, the app skips this stage gracefully and proceeds to discovery questions.
  4. Discovery Questions — Habit-specific prompts
    Based on the user's habit type, the app asks 2–3 targeted questions about common patterns for that habit type. Each question has three response chips: "Yes, this rings true" / "Sometimes" / "No, not really." Selecting Yes or Sometimes adds a corresponding cue to the candidate list. Questions whose topics already appeared clearly in the logs are filtered out automatically.
  5. Build and Rank — Finalise the cue map
    All confirmed candidate cues are shown in an editable, drag-to-reorder list. The user can edit each cue in their own words, delete items (minimum 2 required), add their own (up to 6 total), and drag to rank from least to most triggering (most triggering at the bottom). When satisfied, they tap "Save my pattern triggers."

Completion screen: "You've just done something most people never do — looked honestly at your own patterns. This list is the map. Everything that comes next uses it."

Saving the cue map sets cueHierarchyDone = true, which immediately unlocks Modules 2 and 4, and moves the user to Phase 2 (if not already there).

Weekly Review

The Weekly Review is an existing Module 1 activity that surfaces each week. It asks the user to reflect on the week's patterns at a higher level than the daily check-in — what worked, what was hard, what they noticed. The exact prompts are drawn from the module content library and may vary. The review does not unlock or gate other activities — it is a reflective maintenance habit.

Module 2

Challenge Your Thinking

🔒 Unlocks: cue map complete, or Day 28+

Module 2 teaches the user to slow down and examine automatic thoughts — particularly the permission-giving, minimising, and catastrophising thoughts that precede or follow pattern behaviour. The activity is called Thought Examination and takes 5–10 minutes. It can be initiated directly (from the module screen or the "Examine a thought" quick-action on the habit card) or triggered automatically when a user reports an urge during a Daily Check-In.

The Five-Step Thought Examination

This activity uses five heterogeneous step types — not simply five text fields. Each step is designed to slow the user's relationship to a specific thought.

  1. Capture the raw thought
    Write the thought exactly as it occurred — not a cleaned-up version. The raw thing.
    A text field is provided. The app includes a note: "The uncleaned version is the one that has power. That's the one worth examining." Example chips help users identify common thought forms they might have dismissed: "I deserve this." / "Just this once." / "No one will know." / "I can't cope without it."
  2. Classify the thought type
    What kind of thought is this?
    A six-option grid (single-select), plus an "Other" option that reveals a text field:
    Permission-giving
    I deserve this / just this once / a little won't matter
    Minimising
    It's not that bad / everyone does it
    Catastrophising
    I could never give this up / I have no willpower
    All-or-nothing
    I've already slipped, the day is ruined
    Externalising
    It's stress / my relationship / work that causes this
    Self-condemnation
    I'm broken / weak / hopeless / this is just who I am
  3. Examine the evidence
    Two text fields on one screen:
    What is the actual evidence for this thought? What does your track record say?
    What would you say to a good friend who told you they believed this?
  4. Write an alternative
    Write a more accurate alternative — in your own words, not an affirmation.
    The app's note: "Not 'everything will be fine' — something you actually believe. Something grounded in what your evidence just showed you." An example chip is offered for inspiration: "The evidence from my own history is that once never stays once. The urge will pass in about 20 minutes whether or not I act on it."
  5. Library decision
    Is this one of your recurring thoughts — something that comes up again and again?
    Two buttons: "Yes, save it to my library" or "No, just this once." If saved, the thought, its type, and the user's alternative are added to the Counter-Response Library — a personal reference accessible from the habit card at any time.

Completion message: "Good work. You slowed down a thought that usually moves faster than you can catch it."

After the completion affirmation, the user is offered two options: return to their plan, or open the Behaviour Log to also record the moment they were examining.

Draft saving

If the user exits mid-activity, the app saves their progress to Firestore and resumes from the correct step on next entry — including the thought classification grid selection. The draft is cleared once saved.

Counter-Response Library

The Counter-Response Library is a private collection of thought/alternative pairs built up through repeated thought examination. Users access it via an "My counter-responses" quick-action chip on the habit card (visible once at least one item has been saved). Each entry shows the original thought, its classified type, and the alternative the user wrote. It is read-only from the library view — updating an entry requires doing a new thought examination.

Module 3

Anchor to Your Values

🔓 Always available

Module 3 grounds the programme in values clarification — a core ACT principle. The module has two activities: a one-time Values Inventory (done at the user's own pace, typically in the first few weeks) and a recurring Weekly Values Compass check-in.

Values Inventory

The Values Inventory is a reflective exercise across eight life domains. It is designed to help the user articulate what actually matters to them, assess how aligned their current life is with those values, and observe honestly whether their pattern is moving them toward or away from what they value.

The eight domains

Faith & Spiritual Life
Intimate Relationship & Partnership
Family & Parenting
Friendship & Social Connection
Work, Career & Contribution
Personal Growth & Learning
Health & Physical Wellbeing
Creative Expression & Leisure

What the user does for each domain

For each domain in sequence, the user completes three things:

Reflection text
In your own words, what does [domain] mean in your life, and what would living it well look like for you?
Must be at least 10 characters before proceeding.
Importance & Alignment sliders
Two sliders, each 1–10:
• How important is this to you? (1 = Not at all → 10 = Central to who I am)
• How well are you currently living it? (1 = Not at all → 10 = Fully)
The gap between these two numbers is surfaced to the user. A gap above 5 is described as "There's a meaningful gap here." A zero gap shows "You're living in alignment here." A negative gap (alignment rated higher than importance) shows "Living beyond what you value — that's also meaningful."
Compass direction
Does your current pattern move you toward or away from this?
Toward Neutral Away
Must select one before proceeding. None is pre-selected.

Completion

If any domain was rated "Away," the completion message is: "You can see where this pattern is pulling against what matters to you. That's your compass — not a judgement, just information."

If no domains were rated "Away": "You've mapped what matters to you. As you work through this journey, this is your compass — the life you're building toward."

The inventory data (all eight domains, with their reflection text, scores, and compass direction) is saved to the user's plan and is visible in the Freedom Journey tab as a living document they can return to.

Draft saving

Progress through the inventory is saved after each domain completion. If the user exits mid-way, they resume from the next uncompleted domain on re-entry.

Weekly Values Compass

Once the Values Inventory is complete, the Weekly Values Compass becomes a recurring pending action — surfaced once per week. It is a brief check-in (approximately five minutes) that asks the user to revisit their values in light of the past week.

Three-step compass check-in

Step 1 — Direction check
For each of your values, did this week's actions move toward or away from it?
Each of the eight domains is listed. The user must select one chip for each: [→ Toward] or [← Away]. All domains must be answered before proceeding.
Step 2 — Conditional reflection
Where the compass is pointing away — what's one small thing you could do differently this week?
If all domains were marked "Toward," the app shows: "Nothing pointing away this week — that's worth noticing." The text field is not required in that case.
Step 3 — Committed action
What is one concrete thing you will do this week that moves toward what matters most to you?
A "do" action — calling someone, exercising, praying, doing the creative work you've been putting off.
Required. The app explicitly asks for a behavioural commitment, not a mindset intention.
Module 4

Build Your Guardrails

🔒 Unlocks: cue map complete, or Day 28+

Module 4 translates the insights from the cue map into concrete protective structures. The module's design premise is that willpower fails most precisely when it is most needed — so the work is to engineer the environment and pre-plan responses before high-risk moments arrive. Module 4 has four activities.

Environmental Restructuring

The first Module 4 activity surfaces as a pending action once the cue map is complete. It asks the user to make concrete changes to the physical or digital environment around each of their top three trigger cues.

Introduction
"Change the situation before you need to change your mind. Willpower works worst exactly when you need it most. Making concrete changes to your environment is more effective than relying on willpower in the moment."

For each of the top three cues from the user's cue map, the app shows:

  • The cue text (displayed as a label, not editable here)
  • A text field: "What one concrete change will you make?"
  • Suggestion chips specific to the user's habit type — tapping a chip fills the field as a starting point. Chips can be dismissed.
Vagueness check: If the user's response is under 15 characters or uses generic phrases like "be strong" or "try harder," an inline prompt appears: "Can you make this more specific — a concrete action, not a mindset?"

Completion message: "Good — you've made it harder for the pattern to happen automatically. Up next: a specific plan for each of your triggers."

High-Risk Situation (HRS) Coping Plans

HRS plans are pre-written, specific response scripts — one per trigger cue. The premise is that in a high-risk moment, the decision about what to do should already have been made. The app seeds each plan with the corresponding cue from the user's cue map, so the user is working with their own specific triggers rather than generic scenarios.

Each plan card contains:

  • Situation: Pre-filled from the cue map (editable)
  • What will you do first? — The immediate response step. Minimum 20 characters; the app checks for vagueness: if the response is not specific, it prompts: "The most effective plans are very specific — a concrete action, not a mindset. What exactly will you do?"
  • Who will you contact? — A nominated support person
  • What will you say? — The specific words, pre-scripted

Plans are saved to the user's profile and appear as a living document in the Freedom Journey tab. They are accessible at any time from within the app, so a user in a high-risk moment can retrieve their plan without having to improvise.

Urge Surfing

Urge surfing is a mindfulness-based technique for observing an urge without acting on it. Before the user can access the urge surfing activity, the app shows a one-time educational introduction (shown only on first access):

One-time introduction
"You don't have to fight an urge or give in to it. There's a third option — you can just watch it.

Urges aren't commands. They're neurological events with a natural shape — they rise, peak, and pass, usually within 15–30 minutes, whether or not you act on them. Urge surfing is the practice of riding that arc rather than reacting to it.

The more you practise this, the weaker the urge becomes over time."

The urge surfing activity itself is a three-prompt reflective flow, designed to be done in real time during an urge:

Prompt 1 — Name it
Name it: "I am having an urge to [behaviour]." Write what you're experiencing right now — not as a command to act, just as an observation.
Prompt 2 — Locate it
Locate it: Where in your body do you feel it? Chest, throat, stomach, hands? What is its shape and quality?
Prompt 3 — Watch it
Watch it: Did it rise and then begin to ease? Describe what happened to the urge as you observed it. Was it survivable?

Monthly Lifestyle Audit

The Lifestyle Audit surfaces as a pending action from Day 30 onwards, and then every 30 days thereafter. Its clinical premise is that sustained pattern behaviour often fills a gap in the user's life — the audit aims to make that gap conscious and to begin filling it with real nourishment.

Introduction
"This isn't about optimising your schedule. It's about making sure your life has enough real goodness in it that this habit doesn't feel like the only available relief."

Two-step flow

Step 1A
Looking at your typical week honestly — what is taking from you?
Step 1B (same screen)
What is genuinely nourishing you — not numbing, not obligation, but something you'd actually choose?
Step 2
What two activities could you build into the next month that would genuinely nourish you?

After 30 days, the audit becomes available again as a pending action — the cycle repeats for the duration of the programme.

Module 5

Navigate Lapses

🔒 Unlocks: Day 30+, or any lapse recorded

Module 5 prepares the user for the possibility of a lapse before it happens, and provides structured support in the immediate aftermath of one. The "I had a moment" button on the habit card is always visible and always accessible — it unlocks Module 5 automatically if the user hasn't reached it yet, because the lapse tools are most needed precisely when a lapse occurs.

AVE Education Screen (shown once)

The first time a user accesses Module 5 — either via the Recovery Letter or via a lapse — they see a one-time educational screen about the Abstinence Violation Effect. Its purpose is to inoculate the user against catastrophising after a slip, before one happens.

Verbatim app content
"Before anything happens — read this.

Most people who are changing a deeply ingrained pattern will experience a slip at some point. That's not pessimism — it's what the research consistently shows.

What determines whether a slip becomes a full relapse is almost never the slip itself. It's what happens in the hour afterward.

When a slip happens, the mind tends to catastrophise: 'I've failed. I have no willpower. I'll never change. I might as well give up.' Researchers call this the Abstinence Violation Effect — and it's one of the most dangerous things that can happen after a slip, because it turns a single moment into a sustained return to the old pattern.

These thoughts are not facts. They are the all-or-nothing thinking error at its most damaging. And knowing that — right now, before you need it — is one of the most useful things you can do."

Button: "I've read this — write my letter." After tapping, the AVE screen is never shown again for this habit.

Recovery Letter

The Recovery Letter is a guided writing exercise — the user writes a letter from their current, clear-headed self to their future struggling self. The letter is saved privately and displayed during the lapse flow if a lapse occurs. It is the centrepiece of Module 5's preventive approach.

Introduction text shown to user
"This letter is from you to you — to be read the next time you're struggling. Write honestly. No one else will see this.

Many people find this is the most important thing they write in this whole journey."

Four guided prompts

Prompt 1
Right now, while you're clear-headed, write a letter from your wiser self to your struggling self — for the moment after a slip.

What do you most want to remind yourself about who you are?
Prompt 2
What does the research say about slips? (Hint: they don't have to become relapses. A slip is a data point, not a verdict.) Note: Write it in your own words — this is what you'll read in your hardest moment.
Prompt 3
Which of your values do you want to take the next step toward — even today, even now?
Prompt 4
What do you want to say to your harshest inner voice?

After the four prompts, the responses are stitched into a flowing letter draft that the user can read and edit before saving. The letter is stored encrypted.

Completion: "That's the preparation done. You've given your future self something real to hold onto. Now you know what to do if a hard moment comes — and you have what you need to get back up."

Lapse Flow — "I had a moment"

When a user taps "I had a moment" — available from the habit card at any time, regardless of phase — they enter a structured five-screen flow. The design is deliberately unhurried: Screen 0 asks nothing of the user and simply interrupts the spiral. The forensic questions come later.

0
Stop the Spiral
No questions. Full-screen warm text only:
"First — stop.

Whatever your mind is telling you right now about what this means, about who you are, about whether change is possible — those thoughts are not facts.

One moment is one moment. It is not a verdict.

Take a breath. You're still here. That matters."
Button: "I'm ready to continue." The user cannot be pushed forward; they tap when ready.
1
Recovery Letter + Self-Compassion
The user's own Recovery Letter is displayed — the letter they wrote to themselves for exactly this moment. If no letter has been written yet (possible if the lapse triggered the M5 unlock), a short scriptural or affirmational fallback is shown instead.
After reading the letter
What would you say to a good friend who had just gone through exactly this moment?
Required before continuing.
This response is saved as the user's "self-compassion text" — a record of how they spoke to themselves in this moment.
2
Forensic Analysis
"Let's understand what happened — not to judge it, but to learn from it."

Four sequential questions, each in its own field:

What was the situation just before? Where were you, what were you doing?
What were you feeling emotionally?
What thought arose just before?
Where did your coping plan not hold — and what do you think got in the way?
3
Extract and Recommit
What does this moment teach you about your coping plan that you didn't know before? What would need to be different next time?
Which of your values do you want to take the next right step toward — right now, today?

A "Update my coping plan →" button is available to open the HRS Plans immediately from within this screen, allowing the user to update their plan in light of what the lapse revealed.

✓
Completion
"You came back. You didn't give up. You came back. That is what recovery actually looks like — not a straight line, but coming back every time.

Your plan is updated. Your values are still yours. Keep going."

What happens in the background

  • The lapse is recorded as a session with all four forensic fields and the self-compassion text stored in encrypted form.
  • The user's total lapse count is incremented. This count is visible to the user in their Freedom Journey timeline.
  • If the user was in Phase 1, they move to Phase 3 immediately.
  • Module 5 is marked as unlocked if it wasn't already.

Quarterly Review

From Day 90 onwards, the app surfaces a Quarterly Review as a pending action at Days 90, 180, 270, and 360. The review is a reflective activity that looks at the user's journey over the past quarter — what has changed, what has been difficult, and what remains to be consolidated. The exact questions are drawn from the module content library. This is the primary recurring activity for users in Phase 4 (Maintenance).

Notifications

The app sends local push notifications to support regular engagement. All notifications are scheduled locally on the device — they do not require the server to be reachable. Users can disable these in their device settings.

Weekly Values Compass reminder

Freedom Journey — the User's Programme Record

The Freedom Journey tab appears within the app's Journal section when a user has an active Freedom Plan. It is the user's private record of everything they have done in the programme. It has two views, switchable by a segmented control.

My Journey — Chronological feed

A reverse-chronological feed of every recorded session. Each entry shows the date, a human-readable label for the activity type, and a snippet of the encrypted response. Tapping an entry expands the full content. This gives the user a continuous record of their honest work — visible to them, private to others.

Activity types shown in the feed include: daily check-ins, behaviour logs, mid-point reflections, weekly reviews, thought examinations, cue map sessions, values inventory, values compass check-ins, environmental restructuring plans, urge surfing sessions, lifestyle audits, lapse responses, recovery letters, and quarterly reviews.

My Plan — Module-organised view

A structured view of the five modules, each shown in one of three states:

  • Locked: Dimmed, with the unlock condition shown. Not interactive.
  • Active: Expandable. Shows a "next step" prompt, any pinned living documents for this module, and a list of sessions recorded in this module.
  • Complete: Same as Active, with a completion indicator.

Living documents

The following are shown as persistent, always-current documents within the relevant module section — not just as session history entries:

ModuleLiving document
M1"My Pattern Triggers" — the ranked cue map list
M2"My Counter-responses" — thought/alternative pairs from the library
M3"My Values Inventory" — all eight domain cards
M4"My Coping Plans" — all HRS plan cards (tappable to edit)
M5"My Recovery Letter" — the letter as a read-only card, with an edit button

Summary for Counsellors

What the app does not do

  • The app does not send any user data to a counsellor. All data is private to the user.
  • The app does not assess clinical severity or diagnose. It is a structured self-help tool, not a clinical assessment instrument.
  • The app does not provide crisis support. It directs users to work through their coping plans and existing support relationships.
  • The app does not prevent a user from progressing — all activities can be skipped. The pending actions system surfaces tasks, but the user is never blocked from accessing any module screen directly.

Key clinical touchpoints

  • Lapse disclosure: If a user mentions using the "I had a moment" flow, the app will have walked them through a structured forensic analysis. The four questions in Screen 2 of the lapse flow mirror a standard functional analysis — counsellors may find it useful to ask whether those questions surfaced anything the user wants to discuss.
  • Values work: The Values Inventory and Weekly Compass are concrete ACT-aligned activities. A counsellor can ask which domains the user has marked as "Away" — this is one of the more clinically rich data points the app generates.
  • Thought classification: The thought examination types (Permission-giving, Minimising, Catastrophising, etc.) align with standard CBT distortion categories. Users who have completed thought examination sessions will have a vocabulary for their automatic thoughts.
  • Cue map: The cue map is the user's own ranked list of their specific trigger patterns. It is a useful structured conversation starting point. Users who have completed it may be able to articulate their primary cues with more precision than before.
