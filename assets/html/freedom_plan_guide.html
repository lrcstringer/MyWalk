What is the Freedom Plan?

The Freedom Plan is a structured, private programme to help you understand and change a deeply ingrained pattern of behaviour. It takes place entirely within the app — at your own pace, in your own words, completely private.

It's not a 30-day challenge or a simple tracker. It's a genuine self-study programme built on what works: understanding your specific triggers, examining the thoughts that give you permission, grounding the change in what actually matters to you, and preparing concretely — before a hard moment arrives — for what you'll do when it does.

Most activities take two to five minutes a day. The programme spans at least 90 days before reaching its highest phase, and continues with quarterly reviews for as long as you need it.

Everything you write is encrypted. No one else can read it — not the app, not the team who built it. It's a tool for you.

The Four Phases

The programme moves through four phases automatically. You don't choose your phase — the app tracks your progress and updates it when you've reached the threshold. Each phase unlocks new activities and reflects where you actually are in the journey.

Phase 1

Getting Started

Every person begins here. Phase 1 is about building honest self-observation. You log moments, check in daily, and start building data about when and why your pattern tends to occur. No analysis yet — just noticing.

Phase 2

Going Deeper

You enter Phase 2 when you complete your cue map, after 14 days, or after 14 check-ins — whichever comes first. This phase is about understanding the pattern at a deeper level: the thoughts that precede it, the triggers that drive it, and the values you want to live by instead.

Phase 3

Sustained Practice

Phase 3 begins at 30 days, or immediately if you experience a lapse. This is when the concrete guardrails go in: environmental changes, specific coping plans, lapse navigation tools. The work shifts from understanding to building structure.

Phase 4

Maintenance

Reached at 90 days, once your values inventory is complete. At this stage the daily effort has built habits of its own. The recurring activities become a compass check rather than a scramble — quarterly reviews, weekly values compass, occasional lifestyle audits.

About lapses and phases: If you experience a lapse before Day 30, the app moves you to Phase 3 immediately — not as a punishment, but because you now need the lapse tools. A lapse unlocks the full support framework right away, without waiting for a calendar milestone.

Module 1

Know Your Pattern

🔓 Available from Day 1

Module 1 is where the groundwork is laid. Before any strategy, there has to be honest self-observation. This module is about logging moments and building a picture of your specific triggers — not generic advice about what "usually" causes this kind of pattern, but what causes yours.

The activities in Module 1 build toward the Cue Map — the most important thing you'll do in the programme. Everything after it depends on it.

Daily Check-In

The daily check-in is the primary recurring activity. It takes about a minute. Over time, the pattern of your check-in responses tells you things that individual moments can't — and it's one of the ways the programme measures your progress.

What you'll be asked

Question 1
How are you doing emotionally today?
A slider from 1 (Not great) to 10 (Really well)
Question 2 — appears after you move the slider
Did you encounter your pattern today?
Yes, I slipped I felt the urge but didn't act Clear day

What the app does in response

  • "Yes, I slipped" — the app asks if you'd like to open your behaviour log right now to record what happened. You can also choose "Not now" and just save the check-in.
  • "I felt the urge but didn't act" — the app asks if you'd like to examine the thought that came up (Module 2's thought examination). You can also skip and just save.
  • "Clear day" — saved automatically with a brief acknowledgement.
  • If you rated 1–3 — after saving, the app shows a short note: "It looks like it's been a difficult stretch. Stressful periods are when the plan matters most." With a quick link to your coping plans.

Logging a Moment

The behaviour log is used whenever you want to record a specific moment — either through the daily check-in's "Yes, I slipped" route, or by tapping "Log a moment" on your habit card at any time. You don't have to wait for the daily check-in to use it.

The log captures four things about what happened. The more honest and specific you are, the more useful your cue map will be.

  1. The situation
    "Where were you? What time of day was it?"
    e.g. Home alone, 11pm / Office, after a difficult meeting
  2. What you were doing
    "What were you doing immediately before?"
    e.g. Browsing my phone / Just got home from work / Couldn't sleep
  3. Your emotional state
    "What were you feeling emotionally?" — an emotion name and a slider for intensity (0 = Mild → 10 = Overwhelming)
  4. The thought that arose
    "What thought arose just before? Even a single sentence is fine."
    e.g. "I deserve this." / "Just for a minute." / "No one will know."

The app saves your draft as you go — if you close it mid-way, your answers will be there when you come back.

The cue map is built from these logs. The app needs at least five logged moments before it offers you the cue map activity.

Mid-Point Reflection

Between Day 8 and Day 10, the app surfaces a short reflection — a brief pause before the analysis begins. It appears once and doesn't come back after you complete it.

Question 1
Looking at what you've logged so far — does anything surprise you?
Even if you haven't noticed a pattern yet, that's worth noting.
Question 2
Is there a time of day, place, or emotional state that seems to come up more than once?
Don't force a pattern — just notice if anything stands out.
Question 3
How are you feeling about this process so far?
Honest is good. Frustrated is fine. There are no wrong answers here.

Your Cue Map

The cue map is the most important activity in Module 1 — and one of the most important in the whole programme. It appears as a pending action from Day 14 onwards (once you have at least five logged moments) and takes 15–20 minutes to complete. The result is a ranked list of 2–6 trigger patterns in your own words.

After completing your cue map, Modules 2 and 4 unlock. The cue map is also used to seed your environmental restructuring and your coping plans — those activities are built around your specific triggers, not generic ones.

The five stages

  1. Acknowledgement
    A short reflective screen that marks the significance of what you're about to do. You've been logging honestly for two weeks — that matters.
  2. Read your logs
    All your behaviour log entries are shown in order. Read through them without analysing. Just notice. When you're ready, tap "I've read through them."
  3. AI-suggested patterns
    The app analyses your logs and suggests 2–4 candidate trigger patterns — plain-language phrases based on what's in your specific data. You review each one and keep or remove it. This stage is optional — if the analysis can't run, the app moves straight to discovery questions.
  4. Discovery questions
    A few targeted questions based on your habit type — common patterns for this kind of behaviour that might not have shown up in your logs yet. You select which ones ring true; these become additional candidate cues.
  5. Build and rank
    All your confirmed cues are listed. Edit the wording to be exactly right for you. Delete any that don't fit. Add your own. Then drag to rank — most triggering at the bottom. When you're satisfied: "Save my pattern triggers."

Weekly Review

The Weekly Review is a recurring Module 1 activity that appears each week as a pending action. It takes five to ten minutes and asks you to reflect on the past week at a higher level than the daily check-in — what went well, what was difficult, and what you noticed.

Unlike the daily check-in (which captures individual moments), the weekly review is about stepping back and observing the shape of the week as a whole. It's a habit in itself — regular reflection is one of the things that distinguishes people who make lasting change from those who don't.

The weekly review does not gate or unlock anything else. It's simply a recurring space to be honest about how the week went, and to carry that awareness into the next one.

Module 2

Challenge Your Thinking

🔒 Unlocks: cue map done, or Day 28+

Module 2 is about slowing down the thoughts that give your pattern permission. These thoughts move fast — they're usually gone before you can look at them. Thought Examination is a structured way to catch one, hold it up, and look at it honestly.

You can start a thought examination from the module screen, from the "Examine a thought" chip on your habit card, or automatically when you report an urge during a daily check-in.

Thought Examination — Five Steps

Each step is different — not five text boxes but five distinct ways of engaging with the thought.

  1. Capture the raw thought
    Write the thought exactly as it occurred — not a cleaned-up version. The raw thing.
    The uncleaned version is the one that has power. That's the one worth examining.
    Example chips are offered if you need a starting point: "I deserve this." / "Just this once." / "No one will know." / "I can't cope without it."
  2. Classify it
    What kind of thought is this?
    You select from six types — or add your own:
    Permission-givingI deserve this / just this once
    MinimisingIt's not that bad / everyone does it
    CatastrophisingI could never change / I have no willpower
    All-or-nothingI've slipped — the day is ruined
    ExternalisingIt's stress / work / relationships causing this
    Self-condemnationI'm broken / weak / this is just who I am
  3. Examine the evidence
    Two questions on one screen:
    What is the actual evidence for this thought? What does your track record say?
    What would you say to a good friend who told you they believed this?
  4. Write an alternative
    Write a more accurate alternative — in your own words, not an affirmation.
    Not "everything will be fine" — something you actually believe. Something grounded in what your evidence just showed you.
  5. Decide whether to save it
    Is this one of your recurring thoughts — something that comes up again and again?
    If yes, the thought and your alternative are saved to your Counter-Response Library. If it's a one-off, just save and move on.

Your Counter-Response Library

Every thought examination you save to your library builds a personal collection of your recurring automatic thoughts — and your own answers to them. Accessible from the "My counter-responses" chip on your habit card, it becomes a reference you can read in a high-risk moment without having to think on your feet.

Module 3

Anchor to Your Values

🔓 Available from Day 1

Module 3 is grounded in a simple but important insight: lasting change is easier when you know what you're building toward, not just what you're moving away from. The module has two activities — a one-time Values Inventory and a recurring Weekly Values Compass.

Values Inventory

The Values Inventory takes you through eight areas of life. For each one, you'll reflect on what it means to you, score how important it is and how well you're currently living it, and note honestly whether your pattern is pulling you toward or away from it.

Faith & Spiritual Life
Intimate Relationship & Partnership
Family & Parenting
Friendship & Social Connection
Work, Career & Contribution
Personal Growth & Learning
Health & Physical Wellbeing
Creative Expression & Leisure

For each domain, you do three things

Reflection
In your own words, what does [domain] mean in your life, and what would living it well look like for you?
At least 10 characters required. Write honestly — this is just for you.
Two sliders (1–10 each)
How important is this to you? (1 = Not at all → 10 = Central to who I am)
How well are you currently living it? (1 = Not at all → 10 = Fully)

The gap between the two is shown to you. A large gap is information — not judgement.
Compass direction
Does your current pattern move you toward or away from this?
Toward Neutral Away
You must select one to continue. Nothing is pre-selected.

Your progress is saved as you go — if you close mid-way, you'll resume from the next uncompleted domain.

The completed inventory lives in your Freedom Journey tab as a permanent reference. It's your map of what matters.

Weekly Values Compass

Once your inventory is done, a weekly check-in appears each week as a pending action. It takes about five minutes.

Step 1
For each of your values, did this week's actions move toward or away from it?
You select Toward or Away for each of your eight domains. All must be answered.
Step 2
Where the compass is pointing away — what's one small thing you could do differently this week?
If nothing is pointing away, the app shows: "Nothing pointing away this week — that's worth noticing." The field is not required in that case.
Step 3
What is one concrete thing you will do this week that moves toward what matters most to you?
A "do" action — calling someone, exercising, praying, doing the creative work you've been putting off. Required.
Module 4

Build Your Guardrails

🔒 Unlocks: cue map done, or Day 28+

Module 4 turns what you know about your triggers into concrete structural protection. Its premise: willpower fails most reliably at the exact moment you need it most. The work here is to make the pattern harder to access automatically — and to pre-plan your response so you're not deciding what to do in the middle of a high-risk moment.

Environmental Restructuring

For each of your top three cues, you decide on one specific, concrete change to your environment that makes the pattern harder to access.

"Change the situation before you need to change your mind. Willpower works worst exactly when you need it most. Making concrete changes to your environment is more effective than relying on willpower in the moment."

Habit-specific suggestions are offered as chips — tap to fill the field as a starting point, then make it your own. If what you write is vague (under 15 characters, or phrases like "be strong"), the app asks: "Can you make this more specific — a concrete action, not a mindset?"

High-Risk Situation (HRS) Plans

One coping plan per trigger — a pre-written script for exactly what you'll do when this specific situation arises. The plans are seeded from your cue map, so you're working with your actual triggers.

Each plan answers: what's the situation, what will you do first (specifically — the app checks for vagueness), who will you contact, and what will you say. The plan is written now, when you're clear-headed, so you don't have to improvise later.

Your plans live in the Freedom Journey tab and are always accessible from within the app — including during a hard moment.

Urge Surfing

The first time you open this section, you'll read a short introduction to what urge surfing is and why it works. After that, the three-prompt activity is available whenever you need it.

The idea: you don't have to fight an urge or give in to it. Urges have a natural shape — they rise, peak, and pass, usually within 15–30 minutes. Urge surfing is the practice of observing that arc rather than reacting to it.

Prompt 1 — Name it
Name it: "I am having an urge to [behaviour]." Write what you're experiencing right now — not as a command to act, just as an observation.
Prompt 2 — Locate it
Locate it: Where in your body do you feel it? Chest, throat, stomach, hands? What is its shape and quality?
Prompt 3 — Watch it
Watch it: Did it rise and then begin to ease? Describe what happened to the urge as you observed it. Was it survivable?

Monthly Lifestyle Audit

Available from Day 30, and then every 30 days afterwards. Its premise: many patterns persist because they're filling a gap — something the pattern provides that nothing else currently does. The audit makes that gap visible.

Step 1
Looking at your typical week honestly — what is taking from you?
Step 1 (continued — same screen)
What is genuinely nourishing you — not numbing, not obligation, but something you'd actually choose?
Step 2
What two activities could you build into the next month that would genuinely nourish you?
Module 5

Navigate Lapses

🔒 Unlocks: Day 30+, or any lapse recorded

Module 5 prepares you for hard moments before they happen — and provides structure when they do. The "I had a moment" button on your habit card is always available, regardless of your phase. If Module 5 isn't unlocked yet, it unlocks automatically when you use it, because this is precisely when you need it.

Understanding the Abstinence Violation Effect

The first time you open Module 5, you'll read a one-time explanation of something called the Abstinence Violation Effect — one of the most important things to understand before a hard moment arrives.

Verbatim from the app
"Most people who are changing a deeply ingrained pattern will experience a slip at some point. That's not pessimism — it's what the research consistently shows.

What determines whether a slip becomes a full relapse is almost never the slip itself. It's what happens in the hour afterward.

When a slip happens, the mind tends to catastrophise: 'I've failed. I have no willpower. I'll never change. I might as well give up.' Researchers call this the Abstinence Violation Effect — and it's one of the most dangerous things that can happen after a slip, because it turns a single moment into a sustained return to the old pattern.

These thoughts are not facts. They are the all-or-nothing thinking error at its most damaging. And knowing that — right now, before you need it — is one of the most useful things you can do."

This screen is shown once. The more useful it is, the better — it's there to be remembered in a hard moment, not to be read after one.

Your Recovery Letter

After the AVE education screen, you write a letter from your current self to your future struggling self. It's one of the most important things you'll write in this programme.

The four prompts guide you through it:

Prompt 1
Right now, while you're clear-headed, write a letter from your wiser self to your struggling self — for the moment after a slip.

What do you most want to remind yourself about who you are?
Prompt 2
What does the research say about slips? (Hint: they don't have to become relapses. A slip is a data point, not a verdict.)

Write it in your own words — this is what you'll read in your hardest moment.
Prompt 3
Which of your values do you want to take the next step toward — even today, even now?
Prompt 4
What do you want to say to your harshest inner voice?

After the four prompts, the app stitches your answers into a flowing letter that you can read and edit before saving. When you use the "I had a moment" flow, this letter is shown to you at the beginning — your past self speaking to your present one.

The Freedom Journey Tab

The Freedom Journey tab (in the Journal section) is your private record of everything you've done in the programme. It has two views:

My Journey

A reverse-chronological feed of every session you've recorded — check-ins, logs, reflections, thought examinations, compass check-ins, and more. You can read any entry in full by tapping it. This is your complete honest record.

My Plan

A module-by-module view of your programme. Each module shows its current state — locked, active, or complete — and displays the living documents from that module:

  • Module 1: Your ranked list of pattern triggers (the cue map)
  • Module 2: Your counter-response library — thought/alternative pairs
  • Module 3: Your values inventory — all eight domain cards
  • Module 4: Your coping plans — one per trigger, tappable to edit
  • Module 5: Your recovery letter — read-only, with an edit button

These living documents update as you update them. Your cue map from Module 1 is the same one that seeds your Module 4 plans — everything in the programme is built from the same foundation.

What This Programme Is — and Isn't

What the Freedom Plan doesn't do

  • It doesn't share your data with anyone. Everything you write is encrypted end-to-end and stored privately. No one else can access it — not a counsellor, not the team who built this app.
  • It doesn't assess, diagnose, or treat. The Freedom Plan is a structured self-help tool. It is not a substitute for clinical support, professional counselling, or medical advice.
  • It doesn't provide crisis support. If you are in crisis, please contact a crisis support service. The app is designed for everyday programme work — not acute situations.
  • It doesn't lock you out. The pending actions system surfaces what's next, but you are never blocked from any part of the app. Every module screen is accessible directly. The gating is a guide, not a gate.
  • It doesn't work without honesty. The cue map, the thought examination, the values inventory — all of it is only as useful as the honesty you put into it. The app can hold the structure. You bring the truth.

How it all connects

The five modules aren't separate tracks — they build on each other deliberately:

  • The Behaviour Log feeds the Cue Map.
  • The Cue Map seeds the Environmental Restructuring and Coping Plans.
  • The Values Inventory informs the Weekly Compass and gives language to the Lapse Flow's recommitment step.
  • The Thought Examination draws on the same thought patterns you first noticed in your Behaviour Logs.
  • The Recovery Letter is shown to you during a lapse — written by your past self to your present one.

If you are sharing any of this work with a counsellor or trusted person, the structure of the programme gives you a clear language for it. The cue map names your specific triggers. The values inventory shows what matters to you and where the gap is. The thought examination gives you categories for the automatic thoughts you encounter.

None of that language is required. But it's there if it's useful.

Everything here is private. All responses you write are encrypted end-to-end. No one else can access what you write — not even the team who built this app. This is a tool for you.
